# Count your outs

An **out** is an unseen card that may improve your hand to a likely winner.
{: .ff-page-intro }

## Tonight’s goal

Players should recognise common draws and estimate how many useful cards remain.

## The ten-minute lesson

### 1 minute — ask

Hold A♥ 7♥ on K♥ 9♥ 2♣. Ask how many hearts remain unseen.

There are 13 hearts in the deck. Four are visible, leaving **nine flush outs**.

### 3 minutes — learn the common numbers

| Draw | Typical outs |
|:--|--:|
| Flush draw | 9 |
| Open-ended straight draw | 8 |
| Two overcards | Up to 6 |
| Gutshot straight draw | 4 |
| One card to make three of a kind from a pair | 2 |

“Typical” matters. Some apparent outs may also improve an opponent to a better hand.

### 3 minutes — practise

1. 9♣ 8♦ on 7♥ 6♠ K♣: four tens and four fives = **8 outs**.
2. A♠ Q♦ on J♣ 7♥ 2♠: three aces plus three queens = **up to 6 outs**.
3. 10♠ 9♠ on J♠ 8♠ 2♦: straight and flush possibilities overlap. Do not count the same card twice.

### 2 minutes — introduce clean and dirty outs

- **Clean out:** likely makes you best.
- **Dirty out:** improves your hand but may give an opponent something stronger.

Counting outs is an estimate, not a promise.

### 1 minute — set the challenge

> **Tonight’s mission:** When drawing, estimate your outs before the next card is dealt.

Award the **Out Counter** recognition for the clearest explanation.

## During the game

Ask:

- “Which exact ranks or suits help?”
- “Did you count any card twice?”
- “Could one of those cards help an opponent more?”

{: .table_tip }
Do not reveal folded cards while a hand is live. Count from the cards a player is allowed to know: their hand and the board.

## Hand of the night

Reconstruct one draw. Count the clean outs together and identify any questionable ones.
