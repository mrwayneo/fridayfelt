# Starting hands change with position

The same two cards can be an easy fold in early position and a reasonable hand to play in late position.
{: .ff-page-intro }

## Tonight’s goal

Before entering a pot, every player should ask two questions:

1. **Where am I sitting relative to the button?**
2. **Is this hand strong enough for that position?**

The lesson is not about memorising a large starting-hand chart. It is about learning one dependable rule:

> **Play your strongest hands from early position. Consider more hands only when acting later.**

## The ten-minute lesson

### 1 minute — remember position

Place the dealer button on the table and ask:

> “Who has to make the first decision before the flop?”

Remind everyone that early players decide with the least information, while late players get to see what others do first.

### 2 minutes — introduce three hand groups

For tonight, divide starting hands into three simple groups.

| Group | Examples | Simple meaning |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **Strong** | A–A, K–K, Q–Q, A–K | Comfortable from any position |
| **Playable** | Smaller pairs, A–Q, A–J, K–Q | Often playable, but position and earlier action matter |
| **Speculative** | Suited connectors and weaker suited high cards | Easier to consider late; easy to fold early |

These are examples, not a promise that a hand must always be played.

### 3 minutes — move the same hand

Deal one hand face up, such as **J♠ 10♠**.

Place it in early position and ask:

> “How many players can still wake up with a stronger hand?”

Move the exact same cards to the button after everyone has folded and ask:

> “What useful information do we have now?”

Nothing about the cards changed. The player’s position and the actions before them changed.

Repeat the exercise with:

- **A♣ Q♦** — strong, but earlier raises still matter.
- **7♥ 6♥** — interesting late, usually unnecessary early.
- **9♣ 4♦** — still a fold in any position.

### 2 minutes — use the traffic-light rule

Give everyone this shortcut:

| Position | Traffic light | Tonight’s approach |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Early | 🔴 Red | Strong hands; fold freely |
| Middle | 🟡 Yellow | Strong and clearly playable hands |
| Late | 🟢 Green | Strong hands plus a few more possibilities if nobody has shown strength |

Green does **not** mean play everything. It means late position gives you permission to consider—not automatically play—more hands.

### 1 minute — account for earlier action

Position is only one part of the decision.

- If everyone folds to you, more hands may be reasonable.
- If someone raises, tighten up.
- If there is a raise and a re-raise, continue only with genuinely strong hands.

Say:

> “The more strength other players show, the stronger our hand should be.”

### 1 minute — set the challenge

> **Tonight’s mission:** Before playing a starting hand, quietly label your position red, yellow or green.

Award the **Smart Starter** recognition to the player who best matches their starting-hand choices to their position—even if they do not win the tournament.

## During the game

Use questions that help players make their own decisions:

- “What colour is your position?”
- “How many players are still to act?”
- “Has anyone shown strength?”
- “Would this hand feel different on the button?”

Do not ask what cards they folded during the hand. Let them share afterward if they want to discuss the decision.

{: .table_tip }
A late position does not rescue a very weak hand. Position adds information; it does not change 9–4 offsuit into a strong starting hand.

## Hand of the night

After the tournament, ask:

1. Did anyone fold a playable-looking hand because they were early?
2. Did acting late make a borderline hand easier to judge?
3. Did an earlier raise change anyone’s plan?

Finish with the phrase:

> **Strong cards can play from anywhere. Borderline cards need the help of good position.**

## Parent’s note

Avoid correcting every preflop choice during the tournament. Look for one clear example of position improving a decision and discuss it afterward. Confidence grows faster when players discover the pattern themselves.
