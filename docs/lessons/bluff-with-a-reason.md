# Bluff with a reason

A bluff is not a random big bet. It is a bet designed to make a better hand fold.
{: .ff-page-intro }

## Tonight’s goal

Before bluffing, players should identify the better hands they expect to fold and explain why their betting story is believable.

## The ten-minute lesson

### 1 minute — ask

Ask: “Can you bluff a player who never folds?”

No. A bluff requires an opponent capable of folding.

### 3 minutes — use the four-question test

Before bluffing, ask:

1. **What better hand might fold?**
2. **Does this board support the strong hand I represent?**
3. **Have my earlier actions told the same story?**
4. **Is this opponent likely to fold?**

If the answers are unclear, checking or folding is usually better.

### 3 minutes — compare examples

**Better bluff opportunity:** You raised before the flop, bet an A-high flop, and a scary third suited card arrives. Your story can represent a strong ace or flush.

**Poor bluff opportunity:** Four players saw the flop, the board is low and connected, and two opponents have already called large bets. Too many hands can continue.

### 2 minutes — start small

Beginners do not need many bluffs. Prefer:

- Heads-up pots rather than many opponents.
- Boards that favour your earlier actions.
- Opponents who have shown weakness.
- Missed draws that have little showdown value.

Never bluff simply because “I have not bluffed tonight.”

### 1 minute — set the challenge

> **Tonight’s mission:** Before any bluff, name the better hand you want to fold.

Award the **Best Story** recognition for the most logical bluff attempt, whether it succeeds or not.

## During the game

Ask after the hand:

- “Which better hands were you targeting?”
- “What strong hand did your betting represent?”
- “Did the opponent seem capable of folding?”

Do not ask these questions while cards are live.

{: .table_tip }
Bluffing less often makes the bluffs you choose more believable—and protects a beginner’s tournament stack.

## Hand of the night

Review one bluff or missed bluff opportunity. Was the goal clear? Did the board and previous action support the story?
