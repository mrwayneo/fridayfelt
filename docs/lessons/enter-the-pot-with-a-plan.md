# Enter the pot with a plan

Before putting chips into the pot, know what you want your action to achieve.
{: .ff-page-intro }

## Tonight’s goal

Players should stop treating a call as the automatic “safe” option. They should be able to explain why they chose to raise, call or fold.

> **Do not enter a pot just because calling is cheap. Enter because your hand, position and plan make sense.**

## The ten-minute lesson

### 1 minute — ask

Ask: “If nobody has entered the pot, what are our three choices?”

The answer is **raise, call or fold**. Explain that calling the big blind without raising is called **limping**.

### 3 minutes — compare the choices

| Action | What it can achieve |
|:--|:--|
| Raise | Builds a pot with a strong hand and may reduce the number of opponents |
| Call | Keeps the pot smaller, but gives players behind a good price to join |
| Fold | Saves chips when the opportunity is not good enough |

Calling has a place, especially after another player raises. The lesson is that it should be a decision, not a default.

### 3 minutes — demonstrate

Place A♠ K♦ on the button after everyone folds. Ask what a raise might achieve.

Then show 8♣ 3♥ in early position. Ask whether “it is only one blind” is a good reason to call.

Finally show 7♠ 7♦ after another player raises. Discuss all three options without insisting there is always one answer.

### 2 minutes — give a simple preflop plan

When nobody has entered the pot:

- **Strong or clearly playable hand:** usually raise.
- **Weak hand:** fold.
- **Unsure:** check position and choose the disciplined option.

When someone has already raised, pause and reassess. A hand worth opening is not automatically strong enough to continue against a raise.

### 1 minute — set the challenge

> **Tonight’s mission:** Every time you put chips in voluntarily, finish this sentence in your head: “I am doing this because…”

Award the **Player With a Plan** recognition for the clearest decision, not the biggest raise.

## During the game

Ask:

- “What do you want this action to achieve?”
- “Did someone enter the pot before you?”
- “If you call, how many players may join behind?”
- “Would folding leave you in a perfectly healthy position?”

{: .table_tip }
A beginner does not need to eliminate calling. Success is recognising that a call, raise and fold each create a different kind of pot.

## Hand of the night

Discuss one hand where a player entered the pot. What was the plan? Did later action change it? Would a different first decision have made the hand easier?
