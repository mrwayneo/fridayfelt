# Play the decision, not the result

Good decisions can lose. Poor decisions can win. Poker improvement comes from learning the difference.
{: .ff-page-intro }

## Tonight’s goal

Players should judge a choice using the information available when it was made—not the card that arrived afterward.

## The ten-minute lesson

### 1 minute — ask

Ask: “If you get all-in with A-A against 7-2 and a seven lands on the river, did you make a bad decision?”

No. The result was unlucky; the decision was excellent.

### 3 minutes — separate two scoreboards

| Scoreboard | Question |
|:--|:--|
| Result | Did I win the hand or tournament? |
| Decision | Did I use the available information well? |

The season leaderboard measures results. Poker School also celebrates decision quality.

### 3 minutes — review the season

Ask players to name one skill they used:

- Folding patiently
- Using position
- Choosing starting hands
- Entering with a plan
- Betting for value
- Identifying draws
- Counting outs
- Checking the price
- Reading the board
- Tracking stack size
- Bluffing with a reason

### 2 minutes — handle wins and losses

After a tough hand:

1. Take one breath.
2. Confirm the chips and next action.
3. Ask whether the decision was sound.
4. Let the result go and play the next hand.

After a lucky win, enjoy it—but still recognise whether the decision should be repeated.

### 1 minute — set the challenge

> **Tonight’s mission:** After one important hand, rate your decision from 1 to 5 before discussing the result.

Award the **Season Student** recognition for the player who shows the clearest growth, curiosity and sportsmanship.

## During the game

Use neutral language:

- “What did you know at the time?”
- “What options did you consider?”
- “Would you make the same choice again?”
- “What can you control in the next hand?”

{: .table_tip }
Avoid “You would have won if…” after a disciplined fold. Cards revealed later were not part of the original decision.

## Championship review

After the final event, ask every player:

1. Which lesson changed your game most?
2. What decision are you proudest of?
3. What would you like to learn next season?
4. Which family poker tradition should continue?

Finish by recognising the season champion, then recognise learning, courage, improvement and sportsmanship across the whole table.
