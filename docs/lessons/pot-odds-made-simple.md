# Pot odds made simple

A call is a purchase: compare its price with what you may win.
{: .ff-page-intro }

## Tonight’s goal

Players should look at the pot and the call amount before deciding whether a draw is worth continuing.

## The ten-minute lesson

### 1 minute — ask

Ask: “Would you pay $10 for a fair chance to win $20? What about paying $10 to win $100?”

The reward changes whether the price feels worthwhile.

### 3 minutes — build the pot

Put 600 in the middle. Bet 200.

The next player must call 200 to compete for the new total:

- 600 already in the pot
- 200 opponent’s bet
- 200 call
- **1,000 final pot**

The caller contributes 200 of 1,000, or **20%**.

### 3 minutes — compare with a draw

Use the beginner shortcut:

- On the flop with two cards to come: **outs × 4**
- On the turn with one card to come: **outs × 2**

A nine-out flush draw on the turn is roughly 9 × 2 = **18%**.

If the call requires about 20% of the final pot, it is close but slightly expensive using this simple estimate.

### 2 minutes — keep it practical

For tonight:

1. Count the pot.
2. Count the call.
3. Estimate your outs.
4. Ask whether the reward matches the chance.

Do not chase perfect arithmetic during play. A sensible estimate is enough.

### 1 minute — set the challenge

> **Tonight’s mission:** Before calling with a draw, say privately: “I am calling ___ to compete for ___.”

Award the **Price Checker** recognition for remembering both numbers.

## During the game

Ask:

- “What does the call cost?”
- “What will the final pot be if you call?”
- “How many outs do you estimate?”
- “Are all those outs clean?”

{: .table_tip }
The ×4 and ×2 shortcuts are approximations. They are ideal for learning at the table and can be refined later.

## Hand of the night

Choose one drawing decision. Rebuild the pot, calculate the call percentage and compare it with the estimated chance of improving.
