# Read the board before you act

Pause before every decision and identify what the community cards make possible.
{: .ff-page-intro }

## Tonight’s goal

Players should scan the board for pairs, connected cards and matching suits before looking only at their own hand.

## The ten-minute lesson

### 1 minute — ask

Show A♠ K♦ and a board of A♥ 10♥ 9♥ 8♣ 7♣. Ask: “Is top pair automatically a strong river hand?”

The board allows flushes and many straights.

### 3 minutes — use the three-question scan

Before acting, ask:

1. **Is the board paired?**
2. **Are three or more cards connected?**
3. **Are three or more cards the same suit?**

These questions reveal full-house, straight and flush possibilities.

### 3 minutes — compare boards

| Board | Texture | What to notice |
|:--|:--|:--|
| K♣ 7♦ 2♠ | Dry | Few immediate draws |
| J♥ 10♥ 9♣ | Wet | Straights and flush draws |
| 8♠ 8♦ 3♣ | Paired | Trips and full houses can develop |
| A♣ K♣ Q♣ | Very coordinated | Made flushes and broadway draws |

“Dry” and “wet” are shortcuts for how many draws and strong combinations are possible.

### 2 minutes — notice changing cards

After the turn or river, ask:

- What new hand became possible?
- Did a draw complete?
- Did the board pair?
- Is my previous plan still sensible?

### 1 minute — set the challenge

> **Tonight’s mission:** Before acting on every flop, silently say “paired, connected, suited” and check each one.

Award the **Board Reader** recognition for spotting an important change.

## During the game

Ask:

- “What is the strongest possible hand?”
- “Which draws are available?”
- “What changed on this street?”
- “Does your hand use the board well?”

{: .table_tip }
The strongest possible hand is not necessarily what an opponent holds. Board reading identifies possibilities; betting action helps decide which are plausible.

## Hand of the night

Choose a hand where the turn or river changed the board. What new hands became possible, and who adjusted best?
